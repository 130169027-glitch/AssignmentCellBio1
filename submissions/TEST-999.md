Mitosis metaphase
Human somatic cell after S phase: 46 chromosomes, each made of 2 sister chromatids → 92 chromatids total.

At the metaphase plate, those 46 chromosomes align individually.

Homologous chromosomes do NOT pair in mitosis. There is no synapsis, no bivalent, no tetrad.

Each chromosome’s sister kinetochores face opposite poles (amphitelic attachment), so sister chromatids will separate in anaphase.

Homologs may be near each other by chance, but they are not aligned as a pair. They behave independently.

So: mitosis = 46 independent chromosomes at the plate.

Meiosis I metaphase
After S phase, the cell still has 46 chromosomes, each with 2 sister chromatids → 92 chromatids.

During prophase I, homologous chromosomes pair up in a process called synapsis, forming a bivalent (also called a tetrad).

A bivalent = 2 homologous chromosomes = 4 chromatids.

In humans, there are 23 bivalents.

At metaphase I, those 23 bivalents align at the metaphase plate.

Kinetochore orientation is different from mitosis: sister kinetochores of each homolog face the same pole (mono-orientation). This ensures that in anaphase I, homologous chromosomes separate, while sister chromatids stay together.

So: meiosis I = 23 bivalents at the plate, containing 46 chromosomes total.

Your phrase “23 homologous chromosomes are paired up” is slightly ambiguous. It is better to say 23 homologous pairs or 23 bivalents. There are still 46 chromosomes present, but they are arranged as 23 paired units.

Meiosis II metaphase
The cell has 23 chromosomes, each with 2 sister chromatids → 46 chromatids.

These 23 chromosomes align individually at the metaphase plate, very much like mitosis.

Sister kinetochores face opposite poles, so sister chromatids separate in anaphase II.

So: meiosis II = 23 independent chromosomes at the plate.

Quick comparison
Stage	What aligns at the metaphase plate	Number of units in human	What separates next
Mitosis	Individual chromosomes (each with 2 sister chromatids)	46	Sister chromatids
Meiosis I	Bivalents (homologous pairs)	23 bivalents	Homologous chromosomes
Meiosis II	Individual chromosomes (each with 2 sister chromatids)	23	Sister chromatids
Key takeaway
Mitosis: homologs do not pair. 46 chromosomes align independently.

Meiosis I: homologs do pair. 23 bivalents align. Homologs separate.

Meiosis II: no pairing. 23 chromosomes align. Sister chromatids separate.

The random orientation of the 23 bivalents in meiosis I is also the basis of independent assortment, which is why meiosis produces genetically varied gametes.
